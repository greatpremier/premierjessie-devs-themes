Frequently Asked Questions

Critical answers regarding Johannesburg water bylaws, PIRB Certificates of Compliance, and insurance claims.

What is a PIRB Certificate of Compliance (CoC) and do I need one in Gauteng? expand_more
Yes. Under South African National Standards (SANS 10252-1 and SANS 10106) and City of Johannesburg bylaws, any replacement or new installation of a pressurized water heating system (geyser) or major hydraulic alteration requires a Certificate of Compliance issued by a PIRB-registered master plumber. Insurance companies across South Africa will reject water damage claims if your installation cannot be verified with a registered PIRB CoC number.
How fast can your emergency vehicle arrive at my suburb? expand_more
Our dispatch algorithm positions fleet vehicles continuously around central hubs: Sandton Central, Bryanston, Randburg, Fourways, Rosebank, Bedfordview, and Midrand. Average verified response time is 35 to 45 minutes for urgent flood or burst geyser conditions, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.
How do you bill for emergency after-hours callouts? expand_more
Autopage Plumbing maintains total billing transparency. We charge a flat diagnostic callout rate of R450 within Greater Johannesburg. Once our plumber inspects the problem on site, you receive a precise, written line-item quotation for repairs. If you accept the quotation, the initial diagnostic callout fee is entirely credited toward the repair cost.
Can you assist with water outage contingency systems (JoJo Tanks)? expand_more
Absolutely. We engineer automated water backup solutions designed for Johannesburg Water load-shifting and pipe repair cuts. Systems consist of food-grade JoJo storage tanks (typically 2,500L to 10,000L), automated variable speed booster pumps, mechanical non-return valves, and sediment filtration to safeguard high-end brassware and ensure unbroken water pressure.
What happens if my burst pipe is hidden inside a concrete slab or brick wall? expand_more
We never guess or randomly break open tiles and slabs. Our specialists deploy electro-acoustic ground sensors and non-destructive thermal imaging cameras that trace temperature gradients along the concrete to map the exact leaking rupture within centimeters before any precision access cut is made.
